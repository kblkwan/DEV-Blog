To be honest, after reading the assignment brief, I was lost and had no clue on how to attack this assignment. Despite rereading the brief, I drew blanks. With no clear starting point, I decided to pivot by analysing and breaking down what makes current ‘community hubs’ so successful, and what key components it’s comprised of. The table below contains notes of my research and doubles up as competitor analysis.

<img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kblkwan/DEV-Blog/refs/heads/main/posts/images/BGReasearch.png" width="720">

Drawing from my research, I believe that what makes these applications so popular is that each one helps the user accomplish very specific goals. For example, reddit is useful for finding niche information, trouble shooting problems, and searching for product reviews. In contrast, HelloTalk is a popular language exchange app where it is easy to connect with people with the mutual goal of improving their language skills. 

Using the brief and background research, below is a checklist of key factors that would make a powerful community hub:
- [ ] Addresses the needs of a clear target audience
- [ ] Defines what makes the community valuable to its members
- [ ] Solves a niche problem
- [ ] Supports information sharing and interaction between users
- [ ] Creates ongoing value that encourages users to return
- [ ] Considers how users interact across multiple community hubs using one account
- [ ] Identifies what information would be shared and why
- [ ] Can advertising space be sold?
- [ ] Prioritises functionality and usability over excessive features
- [ ] Provides intuitive and efficient user interactions
- [ ] Design meets industry accessibility standards

This checklist can later be reused throughout the design process as a framework for evaluating whether our decisions continue to align with these criteria. As development progresses, it will also be important to assess whether the proposed functionality remains achievable within the project’s technical capabilities and time constraints.

As the ideation process progresses, I think it would be really powerful to identify potential niches and concepts that are not already dominated by existing community platforms or have a clear monopoly over them.
